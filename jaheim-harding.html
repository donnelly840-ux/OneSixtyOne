Case study / 002

A portfolio is only as current as the last upload. So I built one the photographer runs himself.

Jaheim Harding is a Boston photographer and videographer whose work moves fast: portraits, campaigns, lifestyle, and film. He needed a portfolio with the weight of a gallery and the freedom of a feed.

Most photographers get stuck. The site looks the part on launch day, then slowly falls behind the work, because every new photo means emailing a developer and waiting on a rebuild. The portfolio ages while the photographer keeps shooting.

I designed and built a bespoke site around two ideas: motion that feels like film, and a back end he actually controls. Every frame, transition, and gallery is hand-coded. Behind it sits a headless CMS he runs himself, from a dashboard, with no code and no developer in the loop.

Jaheim Harding  ·  Photographer & Videographer  ·  Boston, MA

The best thing you can build a working photographer is a site he never has to ask you to update.
The build
  • 01Bespoke, hand-built site. Vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. No framework.
  • 02Animated intro loader: the JH monogram zooms into the negative space of the H, and the site emerges from white.
  • 03Full-screen shred hero: each photo splits into four horizontal strips that black out in sequence, hold, then lift to reveal the next.
  • 04Lenis smooth scrolling, parallax, and mix-blend-mode overlay text that stays legible over any photo.
  • 05Collection galleries with an auto-laid-out masonry grid and a lightbox.
  • 06A self-serve headless CMS on Sanity: Jaheim adds, reorders, and publishes photos and whole collections from a hosted dashboard, with no developer and no rebuilds.
  • 07Buildless architecture: the frontend pulls content from the CDN with a hard-coded fallback, so the live site never breaks. Deployed on Netlify.

He shoots, he publishes, the site keeps up. No developer required.
